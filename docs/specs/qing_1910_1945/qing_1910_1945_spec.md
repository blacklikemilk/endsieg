# Qing China, 1910–1945

## Design authority

The maintainer asked for a full Qing overhaul rather than a focus tree: a surviving Qing must open a new Chinese timeline through 1945, while the historical collapse keeps the existing Republic of China content intact.
The Fire Rises is a read-only structural reference for chapter changes, route-driven content and country packages, not a source of copied text or art.
The design follows the pattern already used for Iran and Spain in this repository: one country, three authored chapters, a persistent state layer outside the tree, and decisions and events that carry the politics between focuses.

## Two histories from one starting date

The Qing is playable on the map only from the 1910 start, where it owns China and the Republic of China and the Kuomintang are already defined as rival tags.
The historical path is the existing one: the Wuchang uprising, the provincial declarations, the abdication of Puyi and the transfer of China to the Republic.
The alternate path begins with a dynasty that has actually reformed, negotiated or reasserted itself before October 1911, and it ends with a Chinese state that reaches 1945 under a Qing government.

The two paths must never both run.
When the dynasty survives, the historical revolution events are suppressed and the Republic's own 1912–1916 sequence cannot fire because the Republic has been absorbed.
When the dynasty falls, every Qing campaign event stops and the existing events take over unchanged.

## The four survival routes

| Route | Flag | Who governs | Core conflict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Constitutional Qing | `qing_route_constitutional` | Crown bound by a written constitution and an elected assembly | Cabinet versus court, elections, royal prerogative |
| Authoritarian Qing | `qing_route_authoritarian` | Court government through the Grand Council and the Banner establishment | Reformers, provincial gentry, modern officers |
| Yuan Shikai's Qing | `qing_route_yuan` | The throne survives under Beiyang military tutelage | Court versus Yuan, succession, the army's price |
| Federal Qing | `qing_route_federal` | A negotiated compact between Beijing and the provincial assemblies | Autonomy, taxation, military decentralisation |

Each route is settled once, in the revolution crisis, and then shapes the interwar and wartime chapters through branch locks, decisions, events and ideas.
A route is not a cosmetic label: each one changes who holds power, which decisions are visible, which crises fire and which foreign policy is available.

## Persistent state

| Value | Range | Meaning | Presentation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `qing_legitimacy` | 0–100 | Standing of the throne and its government | Regime spirit tier, decision header |
| `qing_reform` | 0–100 | Depth of institutional reform | Reform spirit tier, revolution check |
| `qing_army_loyalty` | 0–100 | Reliability of the New Army and Beiyang officers | Army spirit tier, coup checks |
| `qing_provincial_trust` | 0–100 | Confidence of provincial assemblies and gentry | Provincial spirit tier, federal route gate |
| `qing_yuan_influence` | 0–100 | Yuan Shikai's personal grip on the army and cabinet | Yuan route gate, coup risk |

The balance of power `QIN_throne_and_assembly` sits on top of these values and carries the day to day struggle between court and assembly.
Its ranges set and clear the flags that focuses read, and its attached decision category holds the moves that push either side.

Chapter transitions fire a milestone event and then swap the tree with `load_focus_tree` and `mark_focus_tree_layout_dirty`.
Values, route flags, project receipts and completed decisions live in country variables and flags, so a swap preserves them.

## The revolution

The revolution is a chain, not a single check.

1. The Railway Protection crisis fires in the summer of 1911 and asks the player to choose between provincial shareholders, foreign creditors, garrison commanders and the court.
2. The Wuchang garrison rises in October. A dynasty with high reform, army loyalty and provincial trust faces a negotiated settlement instead of a war; a dynasty without them faces the historical uprising.
3. If war begins, the court council can still negotiate a settlement while Beijing is held and the army is still loyal, at the price of real concessions.
4. If the dynasty cannot settle, the historical abdication chain runs and the campaign becomes the Republic.

Survival is never granted by one focus.
The settlement gate is a combination of reform, loyalty, provincial trust and the current balance of power, and the player can see each value and what moves it.

## Post-revolution content

Each surviving route has its own 1912–1918 sequence: a constitutional convention or a court consolidation, a cabinet crisis, a provincial confrontation, an army settlement, a foreign recognition question and the First World War.
The interwar chapter opens with the settlement each route produced, then carries the May Fourth crisis, the warlord question, the Nationalist revival, the succession of Puyi and the Manchurian crisis.
The wartime chapter carries the Japanese war from the Marco Polo Bridge through the occupation crisis to 1945, with route-specific government, war economy and diplomacy.

## Japan

Japan reacts to the kind of China it faces.
A constitutional Qing faces commercial and treaty pressure first, an authoritarian Qing faces ultimatums, a Yuanist Qing faces military probing, and a federal Qing faces divide and rule among the provinces.
The Manchurian incident and the open war are not forced into their exact historical form when the Chinese state has changed, but Japanese strategic pressure, the railway zone, the Kwantung Army and the demand for a northern client state are always represented.

## Historical preservation

The existing `ww1_china` chain stays in place.
Its revolution events receive a suppression trigger that only the surviving Qing sets, and nothing else in the chain is rewritten.
The Republic of China, the Kuomintang, the Warlord trees, Manchukuo and the 1936 bookmark content remain untouched.
A 1936 or 1944 start never initialises the Qing campaign because the Qing holds no states in those bookmarks.

## Assets

Focus icons reuse the large vanilla and mod icon pool, chiefly the Chinese, monarchist, industrial, military and diplomatic families already shipped.
Two balance-of-power side sprites reuse native 72x88 art.
National spirit pictures reuse existing idea art.
No new art is required for the first delivery; the icon list in `docs/specs/qing_1910_1945/qing_assets.md` records families that would benefit from bespoke Chinese art later.
