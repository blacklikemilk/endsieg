# Iran, 1910–1945

## Design authority

The maintainer requested replacement of Iran-only era content with bespoke changing trees inspired by The Fire Rises, including layout, events and decisions.
This specification records the implementation owner's selected design within that authorization.
TFR is a read-only structural reference, not a source of modern political content or copied artwork.

## A country that changes chapters

The campaign has three authored focus chapters.
The constitutional Persian state and the Great War occupy the first chapter, the postwar struggle and interwar institutions occupy the second, and Iran's struggle for independence during the Second World War occupies the third.
Each chapter has a central political family, an economic wing, a military wing and a separate diplomatic or regional settlement family.
Only current choices should occupy the political center.
A commitment hides incompatible future branches and recenters the surviving family where useful.
Era changes preserve earned institutions, political decisions and finite project receipts rather than replaying rewards.
Late bookmarks establish the current chapter directly, with historical institutions rather than granting the entire earlier tree's one-off rewards.

## Constitutional Persia and the Great War

The opening problem is an underfunded constitutional government whose provincial authority is contested and whose foreign creditors constrain its independence.
The first strategic choices concern parliamentary authority against palace patronage, domestic taxation against foreign credit, and gendarmerie organization against Cossack command.
The financial route unlocks real provincial projects and builds the state capacity needed to withstand intervention.
The military route turns equipment and training into provincial security, with a choice between negotiated local cooperation and coercive centralization.
The diplomatic family offers defended neutrality, an Entente accommodation or a Central Powers opening without compelling automatic world war.
Wartime requisition, food distribution and local insecurity should generate active choices, with failed relief affecting public support rather than inventing precise famine mortality.
The postwar agreement and the future of the constitutional order lead into the interwar chapter.
Historical dates pace international opportunities but must not leave the player with an empty tree for years.

## Interwar institutions

The central struggle offers Pahlavi centralization, a strengthened constitutional monarchy and a parliamentary republic.
The republic represents a researched alternate constitutional settlement, not a claim that the historical 1921 coup immediately founded a republic.
The historical coup initially leaves Ahmad Shah on the throne, and the Pahlavi monarchy belongs to the later dynastic settlement.
Each route changes government, advisors, authority-building tools and foreign policy priorities.
Economic content covers the treasury, domestic workshops, Khuzestan oil, the Trans-Iranian railway and education.
Military development links the provincial army, arsenals, frontier supply and air or Gulf infrastructure.
Diplomacy balances British influence, northern treaty relations and a third-power opening against regional nonaggression and the Shatt al-Arab settlement.
Regional ambition should provide diplomatic demands and negotiated outcomes before war, followed by controlled integration work rather than automatic foreign cores.

## Iran at war

The opening preserves the government built earlier or establishes the historical Pahlavi state in late bookmarks.
Industry and military wings provide actual facilities and defensive objectives.
Diplomacy offers armed neutrality, Allied cooperation or a dangerous German opening with country existence, war and faction validity checks.
Occupation is conditional on real war or the Allied strategic situation and must not overthrow an alternate republic merely because a calendar date passed.
The response can preserve independence through negotiation or commit to resistance.
The corridor requires Iran to control its Gulf and northern logistics regions and commit industrial capacity or equipment.
Bread supply and provincial cohesion remain player actions, not decorative meters.
The final settlement covers territorial integrity, withdrawal guarantees and northern autonomy negotiations through the end of 1945.

## Interlocking country mechanics

State authority is the primary visible value, on a bounded scale from zero to one hundred.
Foreign leverage is a supporting value on the same scale.
Taxation, provincial agreements, gendarmerie investment, constitutional legitimacy and successful projects improve authority.
Foreign credit and sponsor commitments provide immediate strength while increasing leverage.
Debt settlement and balanced diplomacy reduce leverage at concrete economic or political cost.
Both values appear in decision-category text with their meaning and actionable consequences.
Major institutional reforms depend on successful decisions rather than only focus completion.
At most three package-owned national spirits may coexist, representing the governing institution, economic dependence and a temporary national emergency.

## Decision families

Provincial administration offers negotiated local cooperation, equipment-backed gendarmerie investment and a slower domestic treasury programme.
Economic projects cover Tehran workshops, Khuzestan capacity, Caspian roads and Gulf infrastructure, with ownership checks and one-time receipts.
Foreign policy offers credit, repayment, diplomatic preparation and region-specific negotiations with meaningful refusal consequences.
Wartime relief competes with military supply, while corridor logistics requires control of named Iranian states.
Current chapters and route commitments hide obsolete actions.
Repeated actions require cooldowns and cannot farm factories, equipment, authority or diplomatic rewards without payment.

## AI direction

Historical AI prefers the constitutional opening, cautious wartime neutrality, the rejection of the unratified Anglo-Persian agreement, the Pahlavi settlement and Allied accommodation when overmatched.
Constitutional and republican AI invests in political support and domestic capacity before ambitious demands.
Centralizing AI spends equipment on security and prioritizes defense during war.
German alignment is unavailable without an independent viable Germany and should not pull a weak Iran into an unwinnable offensive war automatically.
Foreign negotiation options use the recipient's strategic situation, relations, faction and war state.
Named audit scenarios cover 1910 weakness, 1916 wartime shortage, 1925 dynastic settlement, 1936 modernization, 1941 Allied pressure and 1945 postwar diplomacy.
AI scores must not be reported as probabilities.

## Presentation and writing

Use balanced branch families with short connectors, staged political reveal, recognizable industrial and military wings, native focus filters and navigation.
Use period Persian and existing HOI4 artwork that genuinely matches the branch instead of copying TFR's VCR font, contemporary flags or global interface skin.
Politics, army, industry and diplomacy should have distinct subject matter and icon families.
Events describe named institutions, local conditions and public choices with serious period framing.
The Great War's civilian suffering requires restraint and verified historical context.
No invented quotations or mortality totals belong in player text.
All supported localisation languages must receive reviewed player text.

## Acceptance

Iran alone receives the new package at every supported bookmark from 1910 through 1945 and crosses chapter boundaries during a continuous campaign.
Other countries keep their existing trees and shared branch behavior.
Old automatic coups cannot override the selected constitutional or republican route.
Transitions cannot reset route choices, duplicate rewards or strand current projects.
Foreign-owned Saadabad, Soviet and British interaction contracts remain valid.
Every focus family has decisions or events, a meaningful payoff, icons, localisation and AI behavior.
Review every visible reveal state, every diplomatic refusal, project ownership loss, subject status, absent sponsor and the historical late-start package.
Source checks and rendered previews must remain explicitly separate from live engine evidence.
