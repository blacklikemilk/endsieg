# WWI campaign timetable

The timetable applies to all four WWI bookmarks (1910, 1914, 1916 and March 1918). Dates are the earliest scheduled points on the historical route. Wars, defeat, ownership and player decisions can delay or change the outcome. The map transfers below use Endsieg's existing states.

## 1918

| Date | Stage |
| --- | --- |
| 29 September | Bulgaria can sign the Armistice of Salonica. Once the peace applies, Bulgarian-held prewar Serbian states return to Serbia. |
| 12 October to 1 November | The Serbian restoration advances through the southern districts and Kosovo, then returns Belgrade to Serbian control as the occupation ends. |
| 28–30 October | Czechoslovakia and the State of Slovenes, Croats and Serbs emerge on 28 and 29 October. The Martin Declaration follows on 30 October. |
| 31 October | Károlyi's National Council takes power in Hungary. Hungary and the eligible Czech and South Slav council governments declare war on remaining Austria-Hungary in the game. These declarations represent the armed breakup of the empire. |
| 1 November | Otto Roth's Banat Republic is proclaimed in Timișoara. The West Ukrainian council takes control of the eligible Eastern Galician states. |
| 3–4 November | Villa Giusti ends Austria-Hungary's remaining wars and applies the existing successor-state settlement. German-Austria initially administers eligible Sudeten districts. |
| November | The German armistice ends the inherited Great War conflicts. Poland receives eligible Polish districts held by Germany and is released if still a German subject. Existing eastern wars and their occupied territory persist. |
| 12–16 November | German-Austria proclaims its republic on 12 November. The Eckartsau declaration follows on 13 November. Serbia begins administering the Banat on 15 November. Hungary proclaims its republic on 16 November. |
| 28 November onward | The Soviet campaign against Estonia becomes eligible after German withdrawal. The Belarusian and Latvian fronts follow in early December. |
| 1 December | Serbia and the South Slav state unite as the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes. The Alba Iulia assembly votes for union with Romania. |
| 14–18 December | The Ukrainian Directory replaces Skoropadsky's Hetmanate from 14 December once German protection ends. Soviet Russia can then open its Ukrainian campaign. If the original People's Republic survived an earlier campaign, it can face the same Soviet advance. The provisional Sudeten districts pass to Czechoslovakia from 14 and 18 December. |
| 24 December | Romanian administration of the eligible Transylvanian states begins, with the arrival at Cluj represented by the existing news event. |

## 1919

| Date | Stage |
| --- | --- |
| Early January | The Soviet campaign against Lithuania becomes eligible. The Ukrainian and Belarusian fronts resolve through military results. |
| 14 February | The Polish–Soviet War can begin as a local war. The player decision and historical AI use the same declaration conditions. |
| 20–21 March | The Vix Note is presented on 20 March. Rejection can bring Béla Kun and the Hungarian Soviet Republic to power from 21 March. |
| 16 April | The Hungarian–Romanian front can open if the Soviet Republic and the Transylvanian border are in place and the war can stay local. |
| 29 May | Hungary can open the Slovak front against Czechoslovakia. The existing withdrawal event becomes available in early July. |
| 17 July | The existing Galician settlement can resolve the Polish–West Ukrainian War. Poland receives the eligible Eastern Galician states. |
| 3 August | The Banat is partitioned. The eastern districts go to Romania and the west stays with the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes. |
| August to 14 November | A real Romanian breakthrough can end Béla Kun's government and place Budapest under temporary occupation. The occupation is scheduled to end on 14 November. |

## 1920–1921

| Date | Stage |
| --- | --- |
| 2 February 1920 | The Treaty of Tartu can restore Estonia's historical independence and frontier. |
| 1 March 1920 | Horthy's regency can follow the provisional Hungarian government. |
| 22 April 1920 | The Warsaw alliance becomes available if the Polish and Ukrainian governments and wars still meet its conditions. |
| 4 June 1920 | Trianon confirms the eligible remaining Hungarian–Romanian border transfers when Hungary is at peace with its neighbors on the affected fronts. |
| 12 July and 11 August 1920 | The existing bilateral white peaces with Lithuania and Latvia become available. Treaty border states still under third-party occupation can be delivered once signatory control returns. |
| 7 and 9 October 1920 | The Suwałki Agreement can close the Polish–Lithuanian War. The separate Vilnius handover can follow from 9 October. |
| 14 October / 31 December 1920 | Finland signs its own Tartu treaty on 14 October. In the game, Petsamo passes to Finland from the exchange of ratifications on 31 December if Soviet Russia controls the district and the two countries are at peace. |
| 18 March 1921 | The Peace of Riga can close a continuing Polish–Soviet War on the historical frontier. A genuine Soviet victory takes precedence and annexes Poland. Central and eastern Ukraine may already be Soviet if the Red Army won there. The western offensive modifier ends when the regional campaign is resolved. |
| August–December 1921 | Burgenland's resistance and the Venice Protocol precede the December handover and Sopron vote. |

## Turkish War of Independence

| Date | Stage |
| --- | --- |
| 30 October 1918 | Historical Ottoman AI can sign Mudros after the collapse of the Balkan front, provided it is still at war with Britain or France. |
| 15 and 19 May 1919 | Greece lands at İzmir and armed resistance begins. Mustafa Kemal takes up leadership of the resistance on reaching Samsun and is available as a commander. |
| 23 April 1920 | The Grand National Assembly opens in Ankara. |
| 22 June 1920 | The larger Greek offensive is represented by the start of the local in-game war. Greece receives an expeditionary force at İzmir if it still holds the landing zone. |
| 10 August 1920 | Sèvres is imposed if the Ankara Assembly is in place and the western settlement remains open. |
| 11 October 1922 | Mudanya can end the Greek front if Turkey has retaken İzmir. |
| 24 July 1923 | Lausanne can follow Mudanya and the abolition of the sultanate. |
