# Era audit — Iran 1925-1935 continuations (`iran_interwar`, 1919-1935)

Scope: `common/national_focus/iran_interwar.txt` (132 focuses), the six settlement continuations below
y=8, and the four shared spines (economy, army, diplomacy, regional) as they serve those continuations.
Cross-read: `common/decisions/iran_oil_settlement.txt`, `events/iran_campaign.txt`,
`common/scripted_effects/iran_campaign.txt`, `common/scripted_triggers/iran_continuity.txt`,
`localisation/english/iran_interwar_l_english.yml`, `docs/systems/iran_great_game.md`,
`docs/systems/crown_and_majlis.md`, `docs/plans/iran_1910_1945/icon_mapping.md`.
Read-only audit. No file outside this document was modified.

The top-of-tree 1919-1925 crisis and the settlement fan-out itself belongs to a parallel audit; it is
touched here only where a defect there invalidates the continuations this audit owns.

---

## 0. Headline: four focus gates can never be satisfied, and they carry most of the tree

`prerequisite = { focus = A focus = B }` is an **AND**. An OR needs one prerequisite block per parent
(`prerequisite = { focus = A } prerequisite = { focus = B }`), which is the idiom vanilla uses
(`SOV_finish_the_five_year_plan`, `SOV_the_ussr_academy_of_sciences`). `iran_interwar.txt` uses **zero**
multi-block prerequisites and uses single-block AND in four places where the parents are mutually
exclusive with each other, so no playthrough can ever meet them:

| Focus | Line | Prerequisite block | Why it is unsatisfiable | What it kills |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `IR2_command_and_cabinet` | 112 | `IR2_reject_the_agreement` **and** `IR2_accept_the_agreement` | the two are `mutually_exclusive` with each other (lines 45, 61) | the **entire political trunk**: the 1921 coup, Reza Khan, landlord settlement, provincial governors, the dynastic question, `IR2_the_final_settlement`, all six settlements and all six continuations (~50 focuses) |
| `IR2_khuzestan_oil` | 1193 | `IR2_domestic_capital` **and** `IR2_foreign_concessions` | mutually exclusive (lines 1158, 1173) | the **entire economy spine from y=2 down**: railway, schools, integrated market, Caspian/Gulf projects, the 1933 oil renegotiation, textile, sugar, credit, registry, university, industrial bank, mining, roads, customs, telecom, urban works, economic capstone (20 focuses) |
| `IR2_gilan_republic` | 174 | `IR2_cabinet_responsibility` **and** `IR2_cossack_brigade` | mutually exclusive (lines 128, 161) | the Jangali → Soviet accord → socialist congress chain, therefore `iran_socialist_support` can never reach the 50 `IR2_socialist_republic` needs; the socialist branch is unreachable three times over |
| `IR2_the_state_in_1935` | 1120 | all six settlement capstones | only one capstone is obtainable, because the six settlements are exclusive | the terminal focus, `iran_interwar_settled` and `iran_campaign.56` are dead |

Net effect today: the only live content is the 1919-1921 agreement fork (4 focuses) plus the four shared
spines. Every branch this audit was asked to review is behind at least one of these gates.

`tools/iran_audit.py` reports "0 problems" and `docs/plans/iran_1910_1945/current_report.md:51-52` claims
"every `prerequisite` and `mutually_exclusive` target exists". Both check *reference* validity, not
*satisfiability*, which is why none of this was caught. Add a check: for each prerequisite block, fail if
any two members are mutually exclusive with each other (directly, or via their `allow_branch` flags).

## 1. Thin, repetitive, or generic-reward branches

41 of 132 focuses have **no concrete reward at all** — only `add_political_power`, `add_stability`,
`add_to_variable iran_authority`, war support/XP, and a flag. All six settlement capstones are in that set,
as are 11 of the 15 diplomacy/regional focuses.

Reward-value outliers (70 days of focus time for one variable tick):

| Focus | Cost | Reward | Problem |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `IR2_trans_iranian_railway` | 70d | `+10 authority`, flag `iran_railway_programme` (never read) | the single largest infrastructure project of the era builds nothing: no infrastructure, no railway, no slots |
| `IR2_league_of_nations` | 49d | `+50 PP`, `+5% stability` | League membership has no diplomatic effect |
| `IR2_shatt_al_arab` | 70d | `+50 PP`, `+5% stability` | the Shatt dispute grants no claim, no opinion, no wargoal |
| `IR2_bahrain_question`, `IR2_gulf_islands`, `IR2_caucasus_interest`, `IR2_mesopotamia_interest` | 49-70d | PP/stability + `custom_effect_tooltip` | the tooltip promises a reaction ("Britain … may refuse to negotiate", "the Soviet Union … will react") and **no effect is applied** — the tooltip is fiction |
| `IR2_regional_ambitions`, `IR2_regional_capstone`, `IR2_diplomacy_capstone` | 35-70d | PP/stability | capstones with no consequence |
| `IR2_royal_authority`, `IR2_household_brigade`, `IR2_restoration_capstone` | 49-70d | authority/stability/XP | 3 of the Qajar branch's 7 focuses |
| `IR2_national_union`, `IR2_nationalist_capstone`, `IR2_red_gendarmes`, `IR2_socialist_capstone`, `IR2_responsible_government`, `IR2_civilian_control`, `IR2_electoral_law`, `IR2_military_academy` | 49-70d | PP/stability/XP | the thin half of four different branches |

81 of the 121 flags the tree sets are **never read anywhere** in `common/`, `events/` or `history/` —
including all four spine flags `iran_army_settled`, `iran_economy_settled`, `iran_diplomacy_settled`,
`iran_regional_settled`, the terminal `iran_interwar_settled`, and both oil flags
`iran_oil_renegotiated` / `iran_apoc_renegotiated` / `iran_oil_settlement`. The 1936 tree's root
`IR3_inheritance_of_the_state` has no prerequisite and no `available`, so the whole 1919-1935 chapter
hands over to 1936 with **no state carried across**: a Qajar restoration, a socialist republic and the
constitutional order all open the same 1936 tree with the same free root.

Repetition, same reward under different names:

- Four focuses each build exactly one `industrial_complex` in a fixed state and nothing else:
  `IR2_state_enterprises` (266), `IR2_textile_industry` (410), `IR2_sugar_refinery` (416),
  `IR2_royal_works` (266). `IR2_royal_works` and `IR2_state_enterprises` even use the same state and the
  same `project_slots`.
- `IR2_pahlavi_army` (`add_manpower reserve`) duplicates `IR2_conscription_law` (`add_manpower reserve`)
  in a different branch, so a Pahlavi run gets the same reward twice for two 70-day focuses.
- `add_research_slot = 1` appears on **11** focuses (`IR2_british_advisers`, `IR2_constitutional_crown`,
  `IR2_parliamentary_republic`, `IR2_civil_service`, `IR2_civil_code`, `IR2_secular_courts`,
  `IR2_iranian_academy`, `IR2_schools_and_workshops`, `IR2_tehran_university`, `IR2_officer_academy`,
  `IR2_american_advisers`). A single run can collect 7 of them on top of vanilla's 5; research slots are
  the scarcest resource in the game and this tree hands them out as filler.

Two `if` guards are always true and therefore decoration: `iran_path_treasury` is set during interwar
initialisation (`scripted_effects/iran_campaign.txt:120`, `date > 1918.12.31`), so the conditional extras
in `IR2_treasury_commission` (+50 PP, +10 authority) and `IR2_industrial_bank` (+50 PP) fire for every
interwar start.

One focus contradicts itself: `IR2_khuzestan_oil` calls `iran_concede_british_small` (raising British
influence) **and** sets `iran_oil_programme`, which is half of the `iran_path_oil_national` trigger that
weights the AI 3× toward `iran_cancel_the_concession`. Developing the field marks you as a nationaliser
while handing Britain more control.

## 2. Missing Great Game / Crown-Majlis links

The balance of power (`PER_bop_*`) is reached by 9 focuses in the whole tree. Inside the six settlement
branches it is reached by **6**, all in the first three branches:

| Branch | `PER_bop_*` pushes | `iran_concede_*` / `iran_reclaim_*` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Qajar (`IR2_qajar_restoration`+) | `IR2_court_ministries` → `push_crown`; `IR2_governors_of_the_crown` → `push_crown_small` | `IR2_press_restraint` → `concede_leading_small` only — **no reclaim anywhere** |
| Constitutional (`IR2_constitutional_crown`+) | `IR2_ministers_before_the_majlis` → `push_majlis`; `IR2_constitutional_army` → `push_majlis_small` | `IR2_budget_control` → `reclaim_leading_small` |
| Republic (`IR2_parliamentary_republic`+) | `IR2_republican_constitution` → `push_majlis`; `IR2_elections_1928` → `push_majlis_small` | **none** |
| Pahlavi (`IR2_pahlavi_settlement`+) | none (correct — the balance is removed under Pahlavi) | **none** |
| Nationalist (`IR2_nationalist_front`+) | **none** | `IR2_economic_independence` → `reclaim_leading_large`; `IR2_oil_revision` → `reclaim_british_small` |
| Socialist (`IR2_socialist_republic`+) | **none** | `IR2_anti_concession` → `reclaim_leading_large`; `IR2_soviet_trade` → `concede_northern_small` |

`docs/systems/crown_and_majlis.md:19` states the nationalist and socialist settlements keep the balance,
but no focus on either path moves it, so the panel sits frozen at `-0.15` (the interwar opening) for the
rest of the game. The Qajar branch can only ever *concede* (its single Great Game call raises British
influence), so a restoration is structurally incapable of recovering sovereignty — the one thing the
restoration narrative is about.

Exact effects to add:

- `IR2_national_union` → `PER_bop_push_crown_large = yes` (its own text: "closes the Majlis and governs
  without elections").
- `IR2_workers_councils` → `PER_bop_push_majlis = yes`; `IR2_labour_law` → `PER_bop_push_majlis_small = yes`.
- `IR2_restoration_capstone` → `iran_reclaim_leading_small = yes` (the restored court revokes the 1919
  agreement's residue).
- `IR2_civil_code` → `iran_reclaim_leading_small = yes` (the 1928 abolition of capitulations is a legal
  act and belongs on the legal focus); `IR2_republic_capstone` → `iran_reclaim_leading_large = yes`.
- `IR2_secular_courts` → `iran_reclaim_british_small = yes` (consular jurisdiction ends when state courts
  take civil and commercial cases — the same act, on the Pahlavi side).
- `IR2_pahlavi_capstone` → `iran_reclaim_leading_small = yes`, so the Pahlavi branch has at least one
  foreign-policy consequence.

### Is the 1933 oil settlement tied to a focus? No — it is implemented four times, unlinked.

`docs/systems/iran_great_game.md:106` says the 1933 negotiation "is handled by the existing oil settlement
decisions, not by a crisis mission." The tree disagrees with itself:

| Path | Gate | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `IR2_oil_renegotiation` (economy, 60,6) | `date > 1932.1.1` | `+10 oil` in 413, `iran_reclaim_british_small`, `iran_campaign.55`, flag `iran_oil_renegotiated` |
| `IR2_apoc_negotiation_1933` (diplomacy, 100,2) | `date > 1932.1.1`, `iran_sponsor_britain_valid` | `+10 oil` in 413, `iran_reclaim_british_small`, `iran_campaign.55`, ENG opinion, flag `iran_apoc_renegotiated` |
| `iran_abadan_company_deal` (decision) | `date > 1932.6.30`, 125 PP | `iran_abadan_deal_signed`, PP, fuel, +10 leverage, +8 British influence, −3 sovereignty |
| `iran_cancel_the_concession` (decision) | `date > 1932.6.30`, 150 PP, authority ≥ 65 | nationalises: −25 leverage, −8 British influence, +6 sovereignty |

Defects: the two focuses are near-duplicates in two different spines, are **not** mutually exclusive with
each other, and neither checks nor sets a flag the decisions read, so one run can sign the settlement three
times (`+30 oil`, three `iran_reclaim_british_small` = −24 British influence) while *also* nationalising via
the decision. `IR2_apoc_negotiation_1933` is gated behind `IR2_third_power_contacts` (the German mission) —
the British oil negotiation requires the German opening first, which is backwards. And a "settlement" that
calls `iran_reclaim_british_small` removes British influence, which is the opposite of what signing a
compromise does in the decision (`+8` influence).

Fix: keep the decision as the mechanic; make `IR2_oil_renegotiation` and `IR2_apoc_negotiation_1933`
mutually exclusive, replace their inline rewards with `set_country_flag = iran_oil_settlement_open` and let
`iran_abadan_company_deal` / `iran_cancel_the_concession` `visible` require it (and drop it once taken);
re-gate `IR2_apoc_negotiation_1933` on `IR2_treaties_of_sovereignty` instead of the German contact; replace
`iran_reclaim_british_small` on both focuses with `iran_concede_british_small` or nothing.

## 3. Alternate-history gaps — the six continuations are equally thin, not equally rich

Each settlement has exactly 7 continuation focuses, so the shape looks symmetric. The content is not:
what separates the branches is flavour text and flags, while the rewards are drawn from the same four
generic constants (PP, stability, authority, army XP). Measured rewards per branch:

| Branch | Concrete rewards | Thin focuses | Foreign links | Research slots | Distinct institution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Qajar | 1 factory (`IR2_royal_works`), 1 army XP | 3/7 | 1 concede, 0 reclaim | 0 | court ministries, household brigade, press licensing |
| Constitutional | 1 research slot ×2, 1 army XP, manpower | 2/7 | 1 reclaim | 1 (`IR2_civil_service`) | electoral law, provincial compacts, budget vote |
| Republic | 1 research slot ×2, 1 infra, manpower | 2/7 | 0 | 2 | civil code, land question, civilian command |
| Pahlavi | 1 factory, manpower ×2, army XP ×3 | 2/7 | 0 | 1 | secular courts, tribal disarmament, state factories |
| Nationalist | 1 reclaim ×2, 1 factory, 1 naval base, 1 research slot, manpower | 3/7 | 2 | 1 | national militia, academy, Gulf claims |
| Socialist | 1 factory, 1 infra, manpower ×2, 1 reclaim | 3/7 | 2 | 0 | workers' councils, land to villagers, red gendarmes |

No branch reuses another's *flags* or text, so the "reuse Pahlavi content" worry is unfounded in the
literal sense. The real problem is the reverse — **Pahlavi content is handed to every path** through the
four shared spines, which carry no `allow_branch` and no settlement flag:

| Focus | What it is historically | Who can take it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `IR2_trans_iranian_railway` | the Trans-Iranian Railway, 1927-1938, the regime's defining project | all six settlements, including the Qajar restoration and the socialist republic |
| `IR2_tehran_university` | founded 1934 by Reza Shah | all six |
| `IR2_customs_reform` | the 1925 tariff autonomy, Reza Shah's first act | all six |
| `IR2_conscription_law` | the 1925 conscription law | all six |
| `IR2_unified_command`, `IR2_provincial_army`, `IR2_arsenal_network`, `IR2_officer_academy` | the 1921-1930 unification and professionalisation of the army under Reza Khan | all six |
| `IR2_land_registry` | the 1929-1931 cadastral law | all six |

And the mirror image — content that belongs to the Pahlavi branch sits elsewhere or nowhere:

- The **1933 oil settlement** and the **German economic mission** (the two foreign-policy acts most
  associated with Reza Shah) live in the shared economy/diplomacy spines, so the Pahlavi branch itself has
  zero foreign policy.
- The **1928 abolition of capitulations** is split in half across two mutually exclusive branches:
  `IR2_secular_courts` (Pahlavi) takes civil and commercial cases from the religious courts and
  `IR2_civil_code` (republic) codifies them. Neither path can complete the reform, and the constitutional
  branch — the one that historically produced the 1928 Civil Code and the 1931 judiciary law — gets
  neither focus. It has no legal or judicial focus at all.
- **The 1935 name change** ("asks foreign chancelleries to call the country Iran") is in the `_desc` of
  `IR2_the_state_in_1935` and is not carried by any focus's effect.

Other concrete branch gaps:

- `IR2_household_brigade` (Qajar court guard) and `IR2_unified_command` / `IR2_pahlavi_army` (national
  army) are not mutually exclusive and carry no penalty, though their texts describe rival command
  structures: a Qajar run can hold a palace brigade and a national army at once.
- The republic has no economic or oil policy: seven focuses of constitutional machinery and not one
  factory, well, refinery or concession.
- `IR2_workers_councils` runs the oil installations "by elected councils" but never touches the oil
  decisions or the concession variables; the socialist branch's only oil act is `IR2_anti_concession`.

## 4. Prerequisite, mutual-exclusivity and pacing problems

Unsatisfiable gates: see §0 (`IR2_command_and_cabinet`, `IR2_khuzestan_oil`, `IR2_gilan_republic`,
`IR2_the_state_in_1935`).

Missing date gates. The tree opens on 1919.1.1 and only six focuses test the date. Nothing stops a player
from taking the 1921 coup in 1919, before the agreement crisis has even resolved:

- `IR2_the_coup_of_1921` (x=28, y=3) — no `available`; requires `date > 1920.12.31`.
- `IR2_reza_khan_minister` — the war ministry follows the coup; add `date > 1921.1.1`.
- `IR2_jangali_congress` — the Gilan congress is 1920-21; add `date > 1920.5.31` (its parent already has it).
- `IR2_soviet_accord` — the 1921 treaty; add `date > 1921.2.25`.
- `IR2_socialist_congress` — add `date > 1926.12.31`.
- `IR2_german_economic_mission` — the German mission is 1935 onward; add `date > 1934.6.30`, otherwise the
  "German" branch of a 1919 tree is available in 1922.

Contradictory pair: `IR2_cabinet_responsibility` (a cabinet answerable to the Majlis) and
`IR2_the_coup_of_1921` (ministers arrested, Majlis not consulted) are both prerequisites of the trunk and
are taken in sequence by design, but they push the balance in opposite directions with no acknowledgement.
Either add a `mutually_exclusive` between `IR2_cabinet_responsibility` and `IR2_the_coup_of_1921`, or make
the coup's reward conditional: `if = { limit = { has_country_flag = iran_cabinet_responsibility }
add_stability = constant:iran_story.stability_cost }`.

Dead conditions: `IR2_qajar_restoration`'s `available = { date > 1924.6.30 OR = { <court flags> } }`
(lines 352-353) and `IR2_parliamentary_republic`'s `iran_provincial_compact` (line 387) are no-ops —
the parent `IR2_the_final_settlement` is already date-gated to 1924.6.30, and
`iran_provincial_compact` is set during interwar initialisation for every 1919+ start
(`scripted_effects/iran_campaign.txt:120`), so the republic's "constitution prepared" gate only really
tests `iran_tax_reformed`.

Two reads of never-set flags: `IR2_tribal_disarmament`'s AI weight uses `has_country_flag =
iran_counterinsurgency` (line 839) and `iran_campaign.54`'s second option uses `iran_decentralized_route`
(event line 345). Neither flag is set anywhere in the mod, so both modifiers are permanently dead.

Pacing. The political trunk costs 35×8 + 70 ≈ 350 days from 1919, so the settlement fan-out opens in
late 1920 while its own date gates hold it to mid-1924; the continuations then cost 70 + 70×6 ≈ 490 days,
putting a branch capstone around 1926. The terminal `IR2_the_state_in_1935` is gated to `date > 1932.12.31`
and costs 98 days, leaving a six-year dead zone in the political column with nothing left to take. The
1930-1935 window in the whole tree contains exactly two oil focuses and three oil decisions. Either move
the settlement gates earlier (the crisis is 1919-1921, not 1924) or add 1930-1935 continuations to each
branch. `IR2_the_state_in_1935` should also not be the only thing standing between 1935 and the 1936 tree
(see §1: 1936 reads none of it).

## 5. Layout

Computed from the file's own `x`/`y` (there are **no** `relative_position_id` declarations, so every
position is absolute and nothing re-flows). Cross-checked with `hoi4_focus_inspect`, which agrees:

```
focuses 132   columns 113 (x 0-112)   rows 15 (y 0-14)   connectors 161
minimum same-row spacing 2 (required 2)   too-close pairs 0   overlaps 0
long connectors 20   max horizontal span 44   max Manhattan span 45
connector crossings 2   connector-through-node intersections 1
```

No two focuses share a cell and no two touch illegally — the grid is clean in that narrow sense. The
problems are structural:

1. **The tree is 113 columns wide.** Siblings are spaced 2 apart everywhere (`minimumSameRowSpacing = 2`),
   so a blank column sits between every sibling pair. Five spine blocks sit in 5-column gutters
   (politics ≤48 → economy 54-68 → army 74-82 → diplomacy 88-100 → regional 106-112). Reading a branch
   means panning; the settlement block alone spans x=0..48.
2. **One orphan cell.** `IR2_road_programme` (line 1397) declares its keys twice:
   `icon` = `GFX_focus_generic_railroad` then `GFX_focus_generic_industry_3`, `x` = 58 then **5**,
   `y` = 11 then **18**, `cost = 10` (the file's only literal). Whichever duplicate the parser keeps, the
   focus is wrong: it either sits at (58,11) with a dead second declaration, or at (5,18) — four rows below
   the tree's floor and 51 columns from its parent `IR2_industrial_bank` (56,10), with
   `IR2_customs_reform` (54,12) then drawing an **upward** prerequisite edge 49 columns across the whole
   economy block. `hoi4_focus_inspect` resolved it to (58,11); the engine's duplicate-key behaviour is not
   something to rely on. Delete the second `icon`/`x`/`y` and the literal cost.
3. **`IR2_the_state_in_1935` sits in the wrong place.** It is at (8,14) — the Qajar axis, directly under
   `IR2_restoration_capstone` (8,13) — although it is the shared terminal for all six capstones at x=8, 14,
   20, 26, 32, 38. Its other five edges are the longest bundle in the tree (12 to 30 columns) and are the
   most likely source of the single reported connector-through-node intersection. Move it to (24,14), the
   centre of the settlement block and directly below `IR2_the_final_settlement` (24,7).
4. **Every settlement diamond is asymmetric.** Children split at parent±2 and the rejoining capstone is
   placed on the **left** child's column, so the right parent's edge always runs 2 columns sideways:
   - `IR2_constitutional_crown` (16,8) → `IR2_electoral_law` (14,10) / `IR2_provincial_compacts_ii` (18,10)
     → `IR2_constitutional_capstone` (14,13) while parents are (14,12) and (16,12).
   - same pattern for `IR2_qajar_restoration` (10,8) → `IR2_court_ministries` (8,10) /
     `IR2_governors_of_the_crown` (12,10) → `IR2_restoration_capstone` (8,13) with parents at (8,12) and
     (10,12);
     `IR2_parliamentary_republic` (22,8) → (20,12)/(22,12) → (20,13);
     `IR2_pahlavi_settlement` (28,8) → (26,12)/(28,12) → (26,13);
     `IR2_nationalist_front` (34,8) → (32,12)/(34,12) → (32,13);
     `IR2_socialist_republic` (40,8) → (38,12)/(40,12) → (38,13).
   Vanilla grammar is ±1 for a split and the capstone on the parent's axis. Move each of the six capstones
   one column right (to 9/15/21/27/33/39), or move both parents to ±1.
5. **`IR2_royal_works` breaks its branch axis.** It is at (10,12) while its only prerequisite
   `IR2_household_brigade` is at (8,11) and its sibling `IR2_press_restraint` at (8,12): the only Qajar node
   off the x=8 line, drawn as an L. Move to (8,12) and shift `IR2_press_restraint` to (9,12).
6. **`IR2_gilan_republic` is 44 columns from one of its parents** — (2,4) against `IR2_cossack_brigade`
   (46,3). That is the tree's maximum horizontal span and reads as a mis-parented focus. Put the Gilan
   branch beside the Cossack branch (x≈44, y=4) or beside `IR2_cabinet_responsibility` (x=4) with the
   Cossack prerequisite dropped (§0 fixes that gate anyway).
7. **Empty regions** (not single blank columns, whole voids):
   - x=32..46, y=1..2 — 30 cells under the agreement fork, between `IR2_majlis_restored`/`IR2_command_and_cabinet`
     (24,2) and `IR2_accept_the_agreement` (48,1); the 24-column edge into `IR2_command_and_cabinet` runs
     through it.
   - x=74..82, y=8..14 — 63 cells. The army spine stops at y=7 (`IR2_army_capstone`), the economy spine runs
     to y=14, so the middle of the tree is a staircase with a seven-row hole beside it.
   - y=15..17 — three entirely empty rows, used only by the `IR2_road_programme` orphan.
   - x=102..104, y=0..4 — the regional block (`IR2_regional_ambitions` at (110,0)) floats 16 columns right of
     the diplomacy root `IR2_treaties_of_sovereignty` (94,0) with no connecting edge at all, because
     `IR2_regional_ambitions` is a root with no prerequisite (it only requires `is_subject = no`, for 35
     days). It reads as a sixth spine that belongs to no one.
8. **Row bands interleave in the regional block.** At y=2 the row is `IR2_gulf_islands` (106),
   `IR2_caucasus_refugees` (110), `IR2_shatt_navigation_rights` (112) — three unrelated focuses, one of which
   (`IR2_caucasus_refugees`) is a child of (108,1) and the other two children of different parents. At y=3
   the row is `IR2_afghan_influence` (106) and `IR2_caspian_fisheries` (108), i.e. the children of
   `IR2_gulf_islands` sit one row below and to the side of a node they have nothing to do with.
